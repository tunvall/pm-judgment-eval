# Response template

Copy this block once per case. For each case, answer independently — don't
look back at your answers on other cases to make them consistent with each
other, and don't discuss your answers with anyone else doing this exercise
until everyone's finished.

There's no research to do and no right answer being tested. This isn't about
what you'd personally decide — it's about what you think separates strong
reasoning from weak reasoning on a decision like this, for anyone facing it.

---

## Case: [case title]

**1. What would distinguish strong reasoning from weak reasoning on this
decision?** (Not "what's the right answer" — what would make you trust
someone's judgment more or less, regardless of which way they ultimately
decide.)

**2. What considerations are essential?** List the specific facts, tradeoffs,
or risks from the scenario that any strong response needs to engage with.
Doesn't need to be exhaustive — name the ones that actually matter.

**3. What conclusions could reasonable people reach?** Is there one clearly
correct call here, or could two people reason carefully and land in different
places? If the latter, what would each side need to have grappled with to
still count as a strong answer?
